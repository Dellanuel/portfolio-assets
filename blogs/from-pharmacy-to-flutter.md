## The Unexpected Path

In 2021, while studying Pharmaceutical Sciences at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, I built my first Flutter app. It was terrible. The state management was nonexistent, the UI was a crime against design, and it crashed on three out of five test devices.

I was hooked.

## Why Pharmacy Made Me a Better Developer

People assume my pharmacy background is unrelated to software engineering. It is actually my biggest advantage.

### 1. Domain Knowledge Is a Superpower

When building DOC!, I did not need a medical consultant to explain drug interactions, prescription workflows, or why a telemedicine app needs different triage levels. I already understood the domain.

This means fewer meetings, fewer misunderstandings, and features that actually match how healthcare professionals think. When a doctor says they need to write a PRN order, I know exactly what data model that requires.

### 2. Pharmacists Think in Systems

Pharmaceutical training teaches you to think about systems — how drugs interact with each other, how one change cascades through a biological system. Software architecture is the same mental model.

A database schema is a system. A state management tree is a system. API dependencies are a system. The skill of tracing cause and effect through complex systems transfers directly.

### 3. Precision Is Non-Negotiable

In pharmacy, a decimal point error in dosage calculation can be fatal. This trained me to be meticulous with data handling in healthcare software. When DOC! processes prescription data, every validation exists because I understand what happens when medical data is wrong.

## The Projects That Matter Most

Every app I have built connects back to a problem I witnessed firsthand:

- **DOC!** — Patients in rural areas waiting days for medical advice that could be delivered in minutes through telemedicine
- **OSCEPRO** — Medical students struggling to find quality OSCE practice material
- **SCA-Q-VAULT** — Healthcare professionals needing better exam preparation tools
- **Safedeals** — The counterfeit drug crisis I saw in pharmacy school, where patients cannot verify whether their medication is genuine

## What I Tell People Who Want to Switch Careers

Your previous career is not wasted time. It is context that makes you irreplaceable in your niche. A finance person building fintech, a teacher building edtech, a pharmacist building healthtech — domain expertise compounds.

The technical skills can be learned. Flutter, Dart, Firebase, Supabase — these are tools. The understanding of a problem space, the empathy for users, the ability to speak the language of a domain — that takes years to develop.

I did not leave pharmacy. I expanded it.
